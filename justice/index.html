Divorce

Divorce through the courts is, without exaggeration, the main legal dispute between spouses. All other disputes stem from it and are connected to it in one way or another. A court divorce is a fairly complex procedure, both in preparing the documents and in the court proceedings themselves.

Child support claims

Here, child support means money paid by one spouse to the other for the upkeep of their minor children. The upside of a child support claim is that it can be filed independently of a divorce suit. In other words, even while still married, one spouse is entitled to claim child support from the other.

Determining the child's place of residence

This dispute is directly tied to divorce. When dissolving a marriage, the judge must settle a number of key questions without which the divorce cannot be granted at all, and this is one of them. By default, the child usually stays with the mother. And as a rule, the spouse seeking the divorce does not try to take the child. Such cases do happen, but very rarely.

Division of property

Another kind of dispute that, by and large, isn't tied to divorce. The law allows joint property to be divided between spouses who are still married, spouses who are divorcing, and spouses who are already divorced. In the last case the law sets a three-year limitation period: a claim for division of property must be filed no later than 3 years after the divorce. After that, it's too late.

Paternity

This is also a category of family disputes, although such disputes can arise between people who were never married. Establishing paternity can come up in two cases: either the man refuses to acknowledge that he is the child's father, or, the other way round, the mother refuses to recognise him as the father and he wants justice. Quite rightly so: a child should know who their father is.

Parental rights

Termination of parental rights: a person deprived of parental rights, whether the mother or the father, loses absolutely all rights and duties towards the child. From the day the court decision takes effect, the former parent becomes a complete stranger to the child. In some cases the only upside is that this person no longer has to raise the child, pay child support or fulfil any other parental duties.
